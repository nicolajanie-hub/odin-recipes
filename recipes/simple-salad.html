Recipe for Simple Salad

Description

It irritates me when people act like eating a salad is a chore, something unpleasant that needs to be tolerated in service of your health, or even a punishment. Salads can be a filling and satisfying meal; I'm pretty confident that most, if not all, would actually be able to find at least one salad that they can look forward to.

If you've tried every kind of salad you can find on a menu and never found something you liked, you might be skeptical... but who says you have to stick to salads with catchy names? Some of my favorite salads have been the result of looking at what's in the fridge and throwing something together that doesn't have a name or a set recipe. So, instead of giving you a recipe for "the salad that'll make you love salad" or something along those lines, I'll show you how I go about making salad.

Ingredients

  • Leafy Greens (You can use basically any leafy greens. If you want a mild base that will keep the focus on the toppings, iceberg lettuce would be my recommendation; if you want something with more flavor and nutrition, romaine lettuce or spinach would be my choices.)
  • Optional: Meat (You can use any meat you like, from canned fish to grilled chicken to ground turkey; you could even use things like leftover curry if you need to use it up!)
  • Optional: Cheese (Use whatever cheese you like if you like it)
  • Optional: Veggies (You can have raw veggies like carrots and bell peppers, or cooked or grilled veggies. I have had cooked potatoes in salad several times, you can get creative and use what you have!)
  • Optional: Fruit (I'm not just talking about tomatoes. You can add basically any fruit, fresh or dried, to salad for a bit of sweetness.)
  • Optional: Nuts (Again: if it's a nut that you like, you can add it to a salad. Walnuts, almonds, pecans...)
  • Optional: Dressing (This can be anything from a store-bought salad dressing to a drizzle of olive oil and balsamic vinegar. It may not even be needed for some salads, especially if you decided to use something else that adds a lot of moisture to the salad.)
  • Optional: Anything else (Seriously, you can get creative! You can add eggs in many forms from hard-boiled to fried or even scrambled. You can add herbs and spices to your salad for extra flavor, or even spicy salsa or pepper if you want a spicy salad. You can also add things like various kinds of beans, canned corn, or canned green beans. Go nuts!)

Basic Steps

  1. Wash and chop the leafy greens and any other ingredients. Some leafy greens, like spinach, may not require chopping, but you should wash all of them off in cold water before you begin. You should also wash basically any other fruits or veggies you plan to add.
  2. Prepare any ingredients that require cooking. This means cooking meat that needs to be cooked and isn't already, as well as grilling anything you want to before it is added.
  3. Add the base of leafy greens to your bowl. Don't worry too much about the amount, but I generally aim to have a little more lettuce than I have toppings in my bowl by the end.
  4. Add everything else to the bowl. I usually add all dry ingredients, then wetter ingredients, then the dressing last, trying to make sure everything has some flavor from the latter two. However, this matters less if you are going to mix it all together before eating.

Additional Notes

  • If you are worried about whether the flavors of ingredients will go together, you can search online for recipes with both ingredients. You may even find a salad recipe using them that you take some inspiration from or even decide to follow completely. Just because you can be creative and do your own thing with salads doesn't mean you have to completely wing it every time!
  • Make sure any leftovers you use are still good. Don't think that spaghetti sauce that's been in the back of the fridge for three months is going to be good if you put on top of some lettuce. Putting leftovers on a salad can be a good way to reuse them, but you still need to follow food safety rules or the consequences will outweigh any health benefit you might be hoping for from the salad.
